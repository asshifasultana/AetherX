REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS

Can SPHEREx see Planet Nine?

Probably not. This model gives a visual magnitude around 21–24, several magnitudes fainter than SPHEREx’s approximate point-source depth of 19.5–19.9 AB per channel. SPHEREx can explore the infrared sky and its sources, but this link does not point to a likely planet detection.

MYTH CHECK

Four different ideas

Planet X is a broad name for a proposed unseen planet. Planet Nine is a specific, hypothetical distant planet proposed to explain some Kuiper Belt orbits. Brown dwarfs are substellar objects that form more like stars. Nibiru is a doomsday story with no credible astronomical evidence.

How to read this map

This visualization is based on synthetic Planet Nine orbit positions that ZTF, DES and Pan-STARRS would not have detected, from Brown & Batygin (2021). It shows where the planet could still be hiding if it exists; it is not a detection or a current ephemeris. A tile’s share is relative probability within that reference population, and the displayed distance and magnitude are medians for the tile.
