        Where SPHEREx looks: just past the rainbow

        Light comes in many wavelengths, and your eyes catch only a thin rainbow slice of them. SPHEREx looks just past the red end, in infrared, and splits that light into 102 colors across six detectors (D1–D6). Hover over a detector to see where it sits, or select one below to combine its images and bring out fainter detail.

        Infrared: invisible to human eyesSPHEREx sees all of this

        The rainbow is the only light your eyes can see. Each wave crest here is drawn to scale: the longer the wavelength, the more spread out the crests.

        μm = micrometre. 1 μm is a thousandth of a millimetre, about a hundredth of the width of a human hair.

        Bar colors are stand-ins. Infrared has no color we can see, so each detector borrows a rainbow color: the shortest wavelengths get violet and the longest get red, like the false colors of a night-vision or thermal camera. Detector ranges are approximate. Each bar summarizes the loaded archive images; it does not draw individual channel response curves.

        Explore this source across wavelengths and time

        Measure the selected sky position in the loaded spectral images. Each point uses the wavelength at that exact position.

        Quick-look extraction uses a small aperture, local background, image quality flags, and variance. Click a point to open its source image.

        Exploratory measurements only: this is not IRSA’s PSF-fit spectrophotometry or a calibrated 102-plane mosaic. Squares are the median of the exposures in each of SPHEREx’s 102 channels (17 per detector); faint dots are single exposures, which may come from different observation times. Hollow dots had near-saturated, nonlinear or bloom-masked pixels in the aperture, so their flux likely reads low; they are left out of the medians. Shaded bands mark water ice (~3.0 μm), methane/PAH (3.3 μm) and CO₂ (4.26 μm).

        For a mission-pipeline spectrum or a complete wavelength cube, use IRSA’s SPHEREx Data Explorer ↗ and its Spectrophotometry or Mosaic Tool.

          MISSION OVERVIEW

          SPHEREx mission

          SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer) launched on March 11, 2025. Its planned two-year all-sky spectral survey will observe more than 450 million galaxies and 100 million Milky Way stars to investigate the origins of the universe.
